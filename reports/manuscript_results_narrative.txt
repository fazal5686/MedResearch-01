RESULTS

Study population
The analysis included 500 patients, of whom 326 experienced the study outcome and 174 did not. The overall readmission rate was 65.2%.

Multivariable analysis
In the multivariable logistic regression model, increasing age was associated with higher odds of readmission (adjusted OR 1.032, 95% CI 1.022–1.043, P < 0.001). Hypertension was also associated with increased odds of readmission (adjusted OR 2.115, 95% CI 1.355–3.351, P = 0.00116).

Previous admissions were associated with higher odds of readmission (adjusted OR 1.342, 95% CI 1.190–1.520, P < 0.001). Longer length of stay was similarly associated with increased odds of readmission (adjusted OR 1.095, 95% CI 1.057–1.137, P < 0.001). Emergency admission was associated with increased odds of readmission (adjusted OR 1.741, 95% CI 1.150–2.642, P = 0.00888).

Diabetes was not independently associated with readmission after adjustment (adjusted OR 1.449, 95% CI 0.908–2.346, P = 0.125).

Model performance
The apparent area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was 0.753. Internal bootstrap validation produced a mean AUC of 0.762, with a bootstrap 95% CI of 0.718–0.799. After correction for bootstrap-estimated optimism, the AUC was 0.742.

Calibration and model diagnostics
The calibration intercept was approximately -0.000 and the calibration slope was 1.000. Variance inflation factors were low, with a maximum VIF of 1.059, indicating no substantial multicollinearity among the model predictors. The model included six predictors and had 54.33 events per predictor.

These findings indicate that the model demonstrated moderate discrimination after internal optimism correction, while the calibration and multicollinearity diagnostics were favorable within this dataset.
